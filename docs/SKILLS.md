# Skills, stats and vocations (design draft)

Status: phases 1 to 4 built. Stats, skills and the check; practice and training; paths and hidden vocations; and the uses of the skills (fighting strength from Might with Melee or Hand with Ranged, hunting for food, field and scriptorium work scaled by checks, and event choices that are checks with success and failure branches). Village control is still to come. Path entry requirements are recorded but not enforced. A tutorial for new players is on the task list.

## Principles

- Eight stats, each capped at 5. Each stat has three skills, each capped at 5.
- A check is 2d6 + stat + skill against a difficulty, rolled with the seeded Oracle.
- A brother's **vocation is a path**, like a class. It is chosen once, it locks him onto that path, and it decides which skills he can grow and which perks he can reach. A brother who does not follow his path does not upgrade himself.
- Skills grow by **practice** (repeated work at the right level) and **training** (a block of time with a teacher or an event).

## Stats and skills

| Stat | Measures | Skills |
|---|---|---|
| Might | Strength, weight, holding ground | Melee, Labour, Holding a gate |
| Hand | Dexterity, aim | Ranged, Hunting, Snaring and tools |
| Hardiness | Body against road, cold and sickness | Endurance, Foraging, Resisting sickness |
| Craft | Making and mending | Building, Smithing and carpentry, Cookery and brewing |
| Wit | Reasoning, reading, reckoning | Scribing, Medicine, Accounts and trade |
| Lore | The old world and the land | Ruins and machines, Tracking and navigation, Old tongues |
| Presence | Social force | Persuasion, Hospitality, Command |
| Faith | Steadiness of spirit | Devotion, Warding (against the Hollow, later), Counsel |

Stats start between 1 and 3, set by the brother's origin (a village child, a soldier's son, a novice from the road). Stats rise rarely (events, exceptional years) and fall after about 55 (Might, Hand, Hardiness).

## The check

- Target numbers: easy 7, hard 10, very hard 13.
- A double is a critical: the result improves by one step.
- Opposed checks (a skirmish blow, a bargain with a tollmaster) compare two totals; the higher wins, ties go to the defender.

## Vocations as paths

A brother's vocation is often **hidden**. It is shown as "?" in the estate until an event reveals it: a dream at prayer, a confession, a bishop's question, a girl in the village who looks at him a certain way, a wound that changes his mind. The reveal sets his path for good.

Until the reveal, a brother is a generalist:
- He can practise any skill, but no skill goes past level 3.
- He has no path perks.
- Events can still hint at his path (for example, a village match offered to a novice who turns out to be a householder).

The player cannot reveal a vocation directly. They can only change where a brother is and what he does, which changes which events can fire. For example, a novice sent to the village with a few brothers for alms may meet a village girl and be revealed as a householder. The risk is that such an event can be defining: it can set a path the player did not plan for. Placement is therefore a gamble, not a switch.

On the reveal, the path locks. Skills he has already built stay where they are, but only path skills grow from then on.

A path has:

- **Entry requirements:** minimum stats, sometimes a building or an existing office (a priest needs a bishop's letter, a monk needs a vow made in the house).
- **Growth:** only the skills listed for the path grow fully. Other skills can still be trained, but do not advance beyond their current level.
- **Perks:** unlocked at set skill levels (for example, a monk's Scribing at 3 unlocks the Illuminator perk).
- **Exits:** events move a brother off his path (leaving the order, marrying, being cast out). The old perks are lost; the skills stay.

### Leaving to marry

A novice whose hidden path is householder may be drawn to marriage. When the reveal comes, he has to leave the house for the village to marry. This is an exit event:
- He departs as a brother who has left the order (not a death), and his place in the choir and rota is filled.
- He joins the village as a householder, with his skills intact and new village perks.
- Later events can bring him back: a visit, a feast, a request for help from the house.
- If the village is under the house's control (below), a marriage keeps him, and the house gains a householder family.

| Householder (married) | Craft, Might | Labour, Building, Cookery | Marriage is an event that moves a monk or a villager onto this path. Spouse and children later. |

The existing six vocations (scribe, farmer, healer, builder, cook, soldier) become the first draft of these. Farmer folds into Householder or Labour work.

## Practice and training

**Practice.** Each week a brother works a job, he earns practice points in its skill, but only if that skill is on his path.
- Points needed rise with each level.
- Work below a brother's level earns little.
- Failed checks earn one or two points.
- A brother off his job for a season keeps his level but earns nothing.

**Training.** A brother can be set to a training block (a season, or a few weeks) in one skill on his path. He does not work his usual job during it.
- A teacher at level 3 or above in the same skill roughly doubles the progress.
- Training costs food and firewood.
- Events can grant training (a travelling master, a daughter house sending a brother to learn).

## Uses

- **Combat:** melee blow = Might + Melee; shot = Hand + Ranged. Stances (hold, press, pray) still change the modifiers. Vocation sets the starting skills, not the strength.
- **Hunting:** Hand + Hunting against a difficulty by season and game. A success brings food.
- **Settlement work:** each job has a base yield and a check that scales it (fields: Might + Labour; scriptorium: Wit + Scribing; infirmary: Wit + Medicine; building: Craft + Building).
- **Exploration events:** a card option can be a check with a stat, a skill, a difficulty and two branches.
- **Traits:** kept as small modifiers and event hooks (a patient brother gets a bonus on long journeys, a proud one a penalty when asking for help).

## Village control

A village near the house can come under the house's control. Control is not a building or a single decision; it is a standing that the house earns or loses, the same way it does with the road.

- **Gaining control:** a run of good dealings (tithes paid, help given, a feast shared), a marriage that binds a householder family to the house, or a settlement by persuasion (Presence) or Command when the lord is weak.
- **What control gives:** a steady tithe of food and firewood, village labour for the fields, a householder cell of marriages and baptisms, and a village deck of events (disputes, festivals, marriages, sickness, quarrels between families).
- **What control costs:** the village asks for justice, protection and feasts. Refusing brings disputes; neglecting it brings the village's standing down.
- **Losing control:** standing falls below a threshold, a rival lord comes, or the village's own people turn on the house. The village's events and tithes stop, and the householders may leave.

Control multiplies the events the house can meet. Each controlled village adds its own deck, drawn on the weekly turn, so the house has more to answer for as it grows.

## Balance check (simulator, 50 years, seeds 7, 11, 23)

Run with `tests/simulate.tscn -- --seed=N --years=50`. The simulator keeps a careful rota by default (about four or five brothers on the fields, two at the woods, one hunting, one in the kitchen); `--passive` leaves every brother on the job his vocation gives.

- **Food.** Passive houses went hungry in 195 to 713 weeks of 2,600. The rota reduced it to zero on all three seeds, with about 10 cold weeks. The default jobs are the problem, not the yields. A starving house is warned by the forecast, but it has no signal that the fields are the fix. The tutorial's estate step is the place to say so.
- **Growth.** Under the rota, 112 to 119 skills reached level 3, but the highest level reached was 3 or 4. Only 8 or 9 brothers in about 70 ever had their path revealed. Hidden brothers stop at level 3, so most of the house never grows past it.
- **Decision for the designer.** Three options: reveal a path by time served (a brother who has worked a job for a year shows his path), raise the generalist cap from 3 to 4, or keep the cap and add reveal events. The numbers do not choose between them.
