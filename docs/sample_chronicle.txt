THE ANNALS OF SAINT COMGALL'S AT THE BURREN


~ The First Year of the House ~

In the spring of the first year, Abbot Ruadhán and seven brothers came over the hill to the Burren, the last house on the western edge of Ireland, where the road runs out at the sea. They found the chapel roofless and the wind coming through it, and dedicated the house to Saint Comgall.

A little later, the brothers raised a granary of stone with walls thick enough to keep out the damp and the mice.

In those weeks, the brothers built a woodshed open to the south, so the wood would dry before the cold.

Not long after, Brother Fintan lay three days in a fever that broke suddenly on the feast of the Holy Cross. The healer had no explanation.

That same spring, a scriptorium was made in the south cloister, with desks by the window and a shelf for the inks.

Before the spring was out, the old guest room was turned into an infirmary, with straw pallets, a boiling pot and shelves for the jars.

Soon after, lightning struck the bell tower, and the bell cracked. It rang flat and sour after that.

That spring, Brother Brendan found feverfew and willow on the north slope and filled the infirmary jars.

That same spring, Brother Fintan set out on pilgrimage to the drowned church at Inishkeel.

In the first summer of Abbot Ruadhán, the feast of Saint Comgall was kept with bread and singing, and half the glen village came.

Brother Fintan came home from Inishkeel, thinner and full of joy, and said he had heard them ring.

Not long after, a family with a sick child was taken into the guesthouse. The child recovered, and the family went on their way in the spring.

In the first autumn of Abbot Ruadhán, the glen village asked for a brother to pray at a deathbed, and the house sent no one. The village did not forget it.

Not long after, a storm came in off the Atlantic in the night. It tore the sheets from the byre roof and flattened the oats in the lower field, and the sea came over the strand white in the dark.

That autumn, Brother Dara finished copying a book of the old world before the damp could take it.

In the first year the fields gave generously, and the house went into winter without fear.

The young man who waited at the gate was gone one morning. No one saw him leave.

In those weeks, riders of the Tollmaster came to the gate, and Abbot Ruadhán invited them to eat in the refectory. They ate the meal, then took the grain anyway.

Not long after, Brother Dara copied out an old-world drawing of a pump, and it was kept in the library.

Soon after, wood began to vanish from the stack, and Abbot Ruadhán ordered a bundle left by the gate each night for whoever needed it.

A little later, Brother Declan was found on the road below the house, beaten and left in the ditch. Everyone knew who had done it.

That winter, Abbot Ruadhán rebuked Brother Dara in chapter for a quarrel with Brother Eoghan. Brother Dara took the rebuke badly.

In those weeks, Brother Ciarán went down to the glen village to make amends.


~ The Second Year of the House ~

Brother Ciarán went to the glen village to make amends, but they would not open their doors to him.

A little later, Brother Brendan found feverfew and willow on the north slope and filled the infirmary jars.

That spring, Brother Eoghan fell ill with fever.

That spring, a boat from the islands was found broken on the rocks below the cliff. Among the wreckage were a sodden psalter and two books in a waxed bag, which the scriptorium dried by the fire.

That same spring, Brother Ciarán went east to search the turbine field.

Brother Ciarán came back from the turbine field bleeding, and for a long time would not say what he had met there.

A little later, a currach came in from the western islands. The house fed the four fishermen, who mended the nets and left a barrel of salt fish in the storeroom.

In the second summer of Abbot Ruadhán, Brother Dara left the house without a word, and took the road south.

That summer, black rot came into the potato beds. The brothers pulled up what they could save and burned the rest.

In those weeks, Brother Fintan fell ill with a fever that the medicine could not break.

That summer, Brother Brendan found feverfew and willow on the north slope and filled the infirmary jars.

In the second autumn of Abbot Ruadhán, someone was stealing from the woodpile. The watch caught a widow and her two sons with their arms full of wood. They were fed, and sent home with a bundle.

A little later, Brother Fintan recast the bell. When it rang for Vespers, people in the glen village came out of their houses to listen.

Not long after, a boy came running from the glen village, and Brother Murtagh went back with him to a deathbed.

Brother Murtagh came back from the glen village. The old woman had died at peace, and the village said they would remember who came.

Not long after, the glen village sent up a cart of turnips and two goats, unasked.

Before the autumn was out, Brother Eoghan lay three days in a fever that broke suddenly on the feast of the Holy Cross. The healer had no explanation.

In the second year the fields gave generously, and the house went into winter without fear.

Soon after, snow closed the pass for three weeks. No one came to the gate, and the brothers kept silence even at recreation.

That winter, a storm came in off the Atlantic in the night. It tore the sheets from the byre roof and flattened the oats in the lower field, and the sea came over the strand white in the dark.

Before the winter was out, no boat came in from the mainland, and the brothers went the whole winter without news of the world beyond the sea.


~ The Third Year of the House ~

In the third spring of Abbot Ruadhán, riders of the Tollmaster came to the gate, and Abbot Ruadhán invited them to eat in the refectory. The captain laughed, but he ate, and he rode away without the grain.

A little later, one of the Tollmaster's riders came back alone and asked to stay. He was received, and took the name Malachy.

In those weeks, Brother Eoghan went east to search the turbine field.

That same spring, Brother Brendan found feverfew and willow on the north slope and filled the infirmary jars.

Brother Eoghan came back from the turbine field bleeding, and for a long time would not say what he had met there.

Before the spring was out, a currach came in from the western islands. The house fed the four fishermen, who mended the nets and left a barrel of salt fish in the storeroom.

In the third summer of Abbot Ruadhán, Abbot Ruadhán rebuked Brother Brendan in chapter for a quarrel with Brother Malachy. Brother Brendan took the rebuke badly.

A little later, Brother Brendan lay three days in a fever that broke suddenly on the feast of the Holy Cross. The healer had no explanation.

In the third autumn of Abbot Ruadhán, a boy came running from the glen village, and Brother Murtagh went back with him to a deathbed.

The young man who had waited weeks at the gate, sleeping in the rain, was at last let in. He took the name Feidhlim.

Brother Murtagh came back from the glen village. The old woman had died at peace, and the village said they would remember who came.

That autumn, wood began to vanish from the stack, and Abbot Ruadhán ordered a bundle left by the gate each night for whoever needed it.

In those weeks, lightning struck the bell tower, and the bell cracked. It rang flat and sour after that.

That autumn, Brother Declan fell ill with fever.

That same autumn, the glen village sent up a cart of turnips and two goats, unasked.

In the third year the fields gave generously, and the house went into winter without fear.

The first snow fell on the chapel roof, and the brothers moved the scriptorium desks closer to the fire.

That winter, Brother Malachy and Brother Declan were set to dig a ditch together after a quarrel. Within a week they were seen laughing at the end of the ditch.

Not long after, riders of the Tollmaster came to the gate, and Abbot Ruadhán invited them to eat in the refectory. The captain laughed, but he ate, and he rode away without the grain.

In those weeks, a trader recognised Brother Malachy from his years as a soldier. Brother Malachy knelt in the mud before the trader and asked his forgiveness. The trader left without a word, but the story went down the road ahead of him.

In those weeks, snow closed the pass for three weeks. No one came to the gate, and the brothers kept silence even at recreation.


~ The Fourth Year of the House ~

Abbot Ruadhán spoke with Brother Murtagh, who listened politely and changed nothing.
