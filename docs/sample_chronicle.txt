THE ANNALS OF SAINT GALL'S AT THE OLD PYLONS


~ The First Year of the House ~

In the spring of the first year, Abbot Fintan and seven brothers came to the ruin at the Old Pylons. They cleared the rubble from the old chapel, sang Vespers under a broken roof, and dedicated the house to Saint Gall.

A little later, the brothers raised a granary of stone with walls thick enough to keep out the damp and the mice.

In those weeks, the brothers built a woodshed open to the south, so the wood would dry before the cold.

Not long after, Brother João lay three days in a fever that broke suddenly on the feast of the Holy Cross. The healer had no explanation.

That same spring, a scriptorium was made in the south cloister, with desks by the window and a shelf for the inks.

Before the spring was out, the old guest room was turned into an infirmary, with straw pallets, a boiling pot and shelves for the jars.

Soon after, lightning struck the bell tower, and the bell cracked. It rang flat and sour after that.

That same spring, Brother Gall found feverfew and willow on the north slope and filled the infirmary jars.

Soon after, a boy came running from the hill village, and Brother Martin went back with him to a deathbed.

Brother Martin came back from the hill village. The old woman had died at peace, and the village said they would remember who came.

That summer, Brother Matthias left the house in the night. The brother sent after him came back alone.

That same summer, Brother Simeon copied out an old-world drawing of a pump, and it was kept in the library.

A little later, a family with a sick child was taken into the guesthouse. The child recovered, and the family went on their way in the spring.

A little later, a trader recognised Brother Hilary from his years as a soldier. Brother Hilary knelt in the mud before the trader and asked his forgiveness. The trader left without a word, but the story went down the road ahead of him.

In the first autumn of Abbot Fintan, Brother Ambrose left the house without a word, and took the road south.

That autumn, Brother João recast the bell. When it rang for Vespers, people in the hill village came out of their houses to listen.

That autumn, someone was stealing from the woodpile. The watch saw nothing, and the wood kept going.

In those weeks, the hill village sent up a cart of turnips and two goats, unasked.

In the first year the fields gave generously, and the house went into winter without fear.

Not long after, riders of the Salt King came to the gate, and Abbot Fintan invited them to eat in the refectory. The captain laughed, but he ate, and he rode away without the grain.

In those weeks, snow closed the pass for three weeks. No one came to the gate, and the brothers kept silence even at recreation.

A little later, one of the Salt King's riders came back alone and asked to stay. He was received, and took the name Gregory.


~ The Second Year of the House ~

In the second spring of Abbot Fintan, Brother Gregory fell ill with fever.

In those weeks, Brother Gall found feverfew and willow on the north slope and filled the infirmary jars.

A little later, Brother Gregory went east to search the pylon field.

That same spring, the hill village asked for a brother to pray at a deathbed, and the house sent no one. The village did not forget it.

Brother Gregory came back from the ruins with two books of the old world, swollen with damp but still legible.

In those weeks, a young man came to the gate and was received as a novice. He took the name Ciarán.

That same spring, Brother Ciarán did penance before the chapter for taking bread from the stores.

That same spring, Abbot Fintan rebuked Brother Hilary in chapter for a quarrel with Brother Gall. Brother Hilary took the rebuke in silence and asked Brother Gall's pardon.

In the second summer of Abbot Fintan, Brother Gregory went down to the hill village to make amends.

Brother Gregory spent two weeks mending roofs in the hill village. When he left, the headman walked him to the edge of the fields.

In those weeks, Brother João set out on pilgrimage to the Shrine of the Drowned Bells.

That same summer, black rot came into the potato beds. The brothers pulled up what they could save and burned the rest.

In the second autumn of Abbot Fintan, a family with a sick child was taken into the guesthouse. The child recovered, and the family went on their way in the spring.

Soon after, someone was stealing from the woodpile. The watch caught a widow and her two sons with their arms full of wood. They were fed, and sent home with a bundle.

Brother João came home from the Drowned Bells, thinner and full of joy, and said he had heard them ring.

A little later, Abbot Fintan rebuked Brother Gall in chapter for a quarrel with Brother Hilary. Brother Gall took the rebuke in silence and asked Brother Hilary's pardon.

A little later, the hill village sent up a cart of turnips and two goats, unasked.

In the second year the fields gave generously, and the house went into winter without fear.

Not long after, Brother Hilary lay three days in a fever that broke suddenly on the feast of the Holy Cross. The healer had no explanation.

Before the winter was out, Brother João took to the infirmary with a sickness of the chest.

A little later, snow closed the pass for three weeks. No one came to the gate, and the brothers kept silence even at recreation.

Soon after, Brother Ciarán did penance before the chapter for taking bread from the stores.


~ The Third Year of the House ~

The young man who had waited weeks at the gate, sleeping in the rain, was at last let in. He took the name Aelred.

That same spring, the hill village asked for a brother to pray at a deathbed, and the house sent no one. The village did not forget it.

In those weeks, a family with a sick child came to the gate, and the gate stayed shut.

In those weeks, Brother Ciarán questioned the abbot in chapter over the barred gate, and was told to hold his peace.

In the third summer of Abbot Fintan, Brother Martin went down to the hill village to make amends.

Brother Martin went to the hill village to make amends, but they would not open their doors to him.

Soon after, Brother Simeon and Brother Gregory were set to dig a ditch together after a quarrel. Within a week they were seen laughing at the end of the ditch.

In those weeks, Brother Aelred lay three days in a fever that broke suddenly on the feast of the Holy Cross. The healer had no explanation.

In those weeks, riders of the Salt King demanded grain at the gate, and Abbot Fintan refused them. On their way out they fired the woodpile.

Abbot Fintan spoke with Brother Ciarán, who listened politely and changed nothing.

In the third autumn of Abbot Fintan, Brother Gall was found on the road below the house, beaten and left in the ditch. Everyone knew who had done it.

Not long after, Brother Gall did not recover from his sickness. He was buried in the brothers' field, facing east.

Not long after, Brother João went down to the hill village to make amends.

Not long after, the feast of Saint Gall was kept with bread and singing, and half the hill village came.

Brother João returned to the house, footsore, and went straight to the chapel.

That same autumn, a family with a sick child came to the gate, and the gate stayed shut.

In those weeks, someone was stealing from the woodpile. The watch saw nothing, and the wood kept going.

In the third year the fields gave generously, and the house went into winter without fear.

That winter, Abbot Fintan confessed before the chapter that he had been wrong to bar the gate, and ordered it left open.

In those weeks, the hill village sent up a cart of turnips and two goats, unasked.

That same winter, Brother Aelred went up to the hermit's cave to pray alone.

Brother Aelred came down from the hermit's cave after a month alone, and sang the Office that night as he had not sung it in years.
