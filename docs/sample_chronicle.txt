THE ANNALS OF SAINT GALL'S AT THE OLD PYLONS


~ The First Year of the House ~

In the spring of the first year, Abbot Fintan and seven brothers came to the ruin at the Old Pylons. They cleared the rubble from the old chapel, sang Vespers under a broken roof, and dedicated the house to Saint Gall.

That same spring, the hill village asked for a brother to pray at a deathbed, and the house sent no one. The village did not forget it.

That same spring, Abbot Fintan rebuked Brother Hilary in chapter for a quarrel with Brother Matthias. Brother Hilary took the rebuke in silence and asked Brother Matthias's pardon.

Before the spring was out, Brother João set out on pilgrimage to the Shrine of the Drowned Bells.

That spring, a young man came to the gate and was received as a novice. He took the name Romuald.

Brother João came home from the Drowned Bells, thinner and full of joy, and said he had heard them ring.

In the first summer of Abbot Fintan, Brother Romuald went up to the hermit's cave to pray alone.

That same summer, Brother Simeon finished copying a book of the old world before the damp could take it.

Brother Romuald came down from the hermit's cave after a month alone, and sang the Office that night as he had not sung it in years.

In the first autumn of Abbot Fintan, someone was stealing from the woodpile. The watch caught a widow and her two sons with their arms full of wood. They were fed, and sent home with a bundle.

In those weeks, a trader recognised Brother Hilary from his years as a soldier. Brother Hilary lost his temper and the trader left cursing the house. The story went down the road ahead of him.

That autumn, Brother João went down to the hill village to make amends.

Brother João spent two weeks mending roofs in the hill village. When he left, the headman walked him to the edge of the fields.

That autumn, black rot came into the potato beds. The brothers pulled up what they could save and burned the rest.

In the first year the fields gave generously, and the house went into winter without fear.

The first snow fell on the chapel roof, and the brothers moved the scriptorium desks closer to the fire.

The young man who had waited weeks at the gate, sleeping in the rain, was at last let in. He took the name Aidan.

That same winter, Brother Simeon copied out an old-world drawing of a pump, and it was kept in the library.

A little later, snow closed the pass for three weeks. No one came to the gate, and the brothers kept silence even at recreation.

In those weeks, the hill village asked for a brother to pray at a deathbed, and the house sent no one. The village did not forget it.

Soon after, Brother Ephrem, a hermit of the gorge, came to the house to end his days among brothers.

In those weeks, Brother Aidan and Brother Martin were set to dig a ditch together after a quarrel. Within a week they were seen laughing at the end of the ditch.

That same winter, lightning struck the bell tower, and the bell cracked. It rang flat and sour after that.

Before the winter was out, a family with a sick child came to the gate, and the gate stayed shut.

Before the winter was out, riders of the Salt King came to the gate, and the house paid them a tithe of grain.


~ The Second Year of the House ~

In the second spring of Abbot Fintan, the Salt King's riders came back for more grain, and were paid again.

Soon after, Brother Ambrose fell ill with fever.

In the second summer of Abbot Fintan, Brother João went down the alms road to the river farms.

Before the summer was out, there was no bread. The brothers fasted though it was no fast day, and some grew weak.

A little later, Brother Martin went down to the hill village to make amends.

Soon after, Brother Matthias went east to search the pylon field.

Brother Martin went to the hill village to make amends, but they would not open their doors to him.

Brother João came home with a cart of grain from the river farms, and their blessings with it.

Brother Matthias came back from the pylon field bleeding, and for a long time would not say what he had met there.

A little later, Brother Simeon lay three days in a fever that broke suddenly on the feast of the Holy Cross. The healer had no explanation.

In the second autumn of Abbot Fintan, the feast of Saint Gall was kept with bread and singing, and half the hill village came.

In those weeks, a young man came to the gate and was received as a novice. He took the name Kevin.

In the second year the fields gave generously, and the house went into winter without fear.

A little later, the Salt King's riders came back for more grain, and were paid again.

That same winter, Brother Aidan and Brother Hilary were set to dig a ditch together after a quarrel. Within a week they were seen laughing at the end of the ditch.

A little later, the hill village asked for a brother to pray at a deathbed, and the house sent no one. The village did not forget it.

Abbot Fintan walked with Brother Kevin in the garden for an hour, and afterwards Brother Kevin was seen at Vespers on time.


~ The Third Year of the House ~

In the third spring of Abbot Fintan, Brother Matthias went down the alms road to the river farms.

Brother Matthias came home with almost nothing, and with word that riders of the Salt King were taking tolls on the river road.

The young man who had waited weeks at the gate, sleeping in the rain, was at last let in. He took the name Brendan.

Before the spring was out, Brother Ephrem went down to the hill village to make amends.

Brother Ephrem went to the hill village to make amends, but they would not open their doors to him.

In the third summer of Abbot Fintan, there was no bread. The brothers fasted though it was no fast day, and some grew weak.

That summer, Brother Simeon fell ill with fever.

That summer, Brother João recast the bell. When it rang for Vespers, people in the hill village came out of their houses to listen.

That same summer, the Salt King's riders came back for more, and this time Abbot Fintan sent them away empty-handed.

In those weeks, Brother Ephrem went down the alms road to the river farms.

That same summer, a trader recognised Brother Aidan from his years as a soldier. Brother Aidan knelt in the mud before the trader and asked his forgiveness. The trader left without a word, but the story went down the road ahead of him.

Soon after, Brother Martin was found on the road below the house, beaten and left in the ditch. Everyone knew who had done it.

That summer, Brother Gall found feverfew and willow on the north slope and filled the infirmary jars.

Brother Ephrem was stopped on the river road by the Salt King's riders. They took the grain, beat him for having so little, and let him walk home.

Soon after, Brother Ambrose and Brother Martin were set to dig a ditch together after a quarrel. Within a week they were seen laughing at the end of the ditch.

In the third autumn of Abbot Fintan, a young man came to the gate and was received as a novice. He took the name Cuthbert.

Not long after, wood began to vanish from the stack, and Abbot Fintan ordered a bundle left by the gate each night for whoever needed it.

Before the autumn was out, black rot came into the potato beds. The brothers pulled up what they could save and burned the rest.

In the third year the fields gave generously, and the house went into winter without fear.

The first snow fell on the chapel roof, and the brothers moved the scriptorium desks closer to the fire.

Not long after, Brother Simeon finished copying a book of the old world before the damp could take it.

That winter, Brother Ephrem did penance before the chapter for taking bread from the stores.

Soon after, a boy came running from the hill village, and Brother Gall went back with him to a deathbed.


~ The Fourth Year of the House ~

Brother Gall reached the hill village too late, and could only help bury the dead. He came home quiet.
